A good clip matters more than any algorithm. Setting up the phone takes about a minute, and in return you get more accurate results and fewer rejected clips.

## 1. Ten things to remember

1. **Landscape, phone held still.** Use a tripod, a phone clamp, or prop the phone up firmly; don't hold it by hand. Mounting ideas: clamp it to the net post, clamp it to the fence around the court, or set it on a shelf or railing on the back wall.
2. **Use the 1× lens.** Don't use 0.5×: the ultra-wide lens distorts people near the edge of the frame.
3. **Badminton:** put the phone behind the court and up high (back wall or fence), or at the **back-corner diagonal**. Film from the side only when there is at least 4 m of free space.
4. **Pickleball:** put the phone on the sideline fence, level with where you hit, 3–5 m away; or on the diagonal.
5. **Can't put the phone at the side?** Use the behind or diagonal view. The app tells you which points that view cannot measure (see section 4).
6. **Whole body and racket in frame.** The player should fill about ½–⅔ of the frame height, with space above the racket at full reach and below the feet. When you record in the app, the framing light checks this for you.
7. **Record at 60 fps.** In-app recording is always 60 fps. If you use the Camera app, choose 1080p HD at 60 fps and **turn off "Auto FPS"** (see section 5).
8. **Even light, no backlight.** Don't film toward windows or lights shining into the lens. Outdoors: keep the sun behind the camera.
9. **Only the player in frame, if possible.** If other people appear, the app asks you to tap the right player.
10. **One stroke, 5–10 reps, at most 30 seconds.** Ask a friend to feed you shuttles or balls; serves you can practice on your own.

## 2. The three camera views

| Code | Name in the app | Where to put the phone | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S** | Side view | On the sideline, **on your racket-hand side**, level with where you hit, square to the hitting direction | Front-to-back movement: contact in front of the body, weight transfer, knee bend, forward lean |
| **B** | Behind view | Behind the baseline, near the middle of your half, looking along the hitting direction | Side-to-side movement: stance width, shoulder and hip turn, racket finishing across the body |
| **D** | Back-corner diagonal | At the back corner, about 45° to the hitting direction, preferably on your racket-hand side | A compromise: measures most points, but many only as estimates |

A front view (F, phone facing the player) gives the same results as the behind view.

## 3. Recommended view per skill

| Sport | Skill | Best | Also OK |
|---|---|---|---|
| Badminton | Forehand overhead clear | **D** (back-corner diagonal, raised) | B → S |
| Badminton | Smash | **D** | B → S |
| Badminton | Low backhand serve | **S** | D |
| Pickleball | Serve | **S** (from the sideline fence) | D |
| Pickleball | Dink | **S** | D |
| Pickleball | Third-shot drop | **S** | D → B |

The skill picker always shows the recommended view. More skills will be added over time.

### 3.1 Badminton

**Back-corner diagonal (D), for clears and smashes**
- Put the phone at the back corner, about 45°, preferably on your racket-hand side.
- Height 1.5–2.5 m: a railing, a shelf on the back wall, or clamped to the fence.
- About 4–6 m from the player. The diagonal is usually longer than the space behind the baseline, so this is the easiest way to get enough distance in a crowded hall.

**Behind view (B)**
- Put the phone behind the baseline, near the middle of your half, 1.5–2.5 m high.
- If the wall is too close, mount the phone higher or switch to the diagonal. The framing light says "Move back" if you fill too much of the frame.

**Side view (S), for the low backhand serve**
- Put the phone on the sideline, on your racket-hand side, level with where you serve, 4–6 m away.
- Height: waist to chest (about 1–1.3 m).
- For clears and smashes, use this view only when there is at least 4 m of free space beside the court and nobody walking through.

### 3.2 Pickleball

**Side view (S), for serves, dinks and third-shot drops**
- Clamp the phone to the sideline fence, on your racket-hand side, level with where you hit: level with the baseline for serves, with the kitchen line for dinks, with where you stand for drops.
- 3–5 m from the player, at waist-to-chest height (about 1–1.3 m).

**Diagonal (D)**
- At the corner of the court, about 45°, 3–5 m away. Use it when the side fence is too far or blocked.

**Behind view (B), third-shot drop only**
- Behind the baseline, near the middle, as high as you can.

## 4. When you can't film from the side: what each view cannot measure

Each view only sees some movements clearly. You still get an analysis, but the points below are marked "Can't assess from this video" or scored only as estimates. Once you pick a view, the app shows the exact list for the skill you are practicing.

| View | Cannot measure | Rough estimate only |
|---|---|---|
| **S** – side | Stance width (side to side); whether the racket finishes across the body; left–right balance; sideways crossover steps. For dinks: whether you move your feet instead of reaching | Shoulder and hip turn |
| **B** – behind (and **F** – front) | Whether contact is in front of the body; weight transfer from back to front; forward lean; front-to-back foot spacing (staggered stance, lunge length) | Length of the backswing and follow-through, because depth is lost |
| **D** – diagonal | Shoulder and hip turn (side-on turn, trunk rotation, facing the net) | Most other points |

**In every view**, the app cannot measure: your grip, the shuttle's or ball's flight and landing spot, whether your foot touches a line, or whether you are on the balls of your feet. Racket-face angle is judged by the AI from still images only, so it may be uncertain. Elbow angle is always an estimate, and the contact moment is estimated from wrist speed.

## 5. Setting up 60 fps on iPhone

**Recording in RacketCoach:** nothing to set. The app always records 1080p at 60 fps, with no audio, for up to 30 seconds.

**Recording with the Camera app, then importing:**
1. Open **Settings › Camera › Record Video**.
2. Choose **1080p HD at 60 fps**.
3. On the same screen, find **Auto FPS** and set it to **Off**. If it stays on, the iPhone drops to 24 or 30 fps in dim halls, and fast strokes blur.
4. Open the Camera app, choose **Video**, and check that the corner of the screen shows **HD · 60**.
5. No need for slow motion (Slo-mo), and don't use Cinematic mode. 60 fps is enough.

**Clips received through Zalo or Messenger:** messaging apps usually compress video and lower the frame rate. If possible, ask the person who filmed it to send the original as a **file** rather than as a video. Clips under 30 fps are still analyzed but less accurately; clips under 24 fps are rejected.

**Length:** in-app recordings are at most 30 seconds and need no trimming. Clips imported from Photos or Files are trimmed to 3–30 seconds in the app.

## 6. Light and clothing

- Bright, even light. Don't film toward windows, doors or lights shining into the lens.
- Outdoors: keep the sun behind the camera and out of the frame.
- Wear clothes that contrast with the background; shorts or fitted pants make your knees easy to see.

## 7. One stroke per clip

- Film only **one** stroke per clip, repeated 5–10 times in at most 30 seconds. For example: only clears, no smashes mixed in.
- For clears, smashes, dinks and drops: ask a friend to feed you shuttles or balls at a steady pace.
- Serves: you can practice on your own.
- Did another stroke sneak in? No problem. On the **"Confirm strokes"** screen, untick any wrong strokes before you submit.

## 8. Filming other people

- Only film and analyze someone else **with their consent**. The app asks you to confirm this.
- If the person in the clip is **under 16**, you must be their parent or legal guardian, or have that person's consent. The app then sends pose data only and **no images**.
- Keep strangers out of the frame. The app automatically masks everyone except the player and blurs faces on a best-effort basis, which cannot be guaranteed in every case.

## 9. When a clip is rejected

A clip rejected at the checking stage **does not use a credit**. Fix the issue and try again.

| The app says | What to do |
|---|---|
| No strokes detected | Repeat one stroke 5–10 times; keep your whole body in frame; record at 60 fps |
| Player too small | Move the phone closer; the player should fill ½–⅔ of the frame height |
| Player hard to see | Add light and avoid backlight; keep the phone still; keep the whole body in frame |
| Clip too short or too long | Keep the clip between 3 and 30 seconds |
| Lost track of the player | Keep only one person in frame; keep the whole body visible throughout |
| Frame rate too low | Record again at 60 fps with "Auto FPS" turned off |
| Wrong sport or skill | Pick the right skill before recording |
